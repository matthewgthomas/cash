Cash assistance design helper

This tool supports with two key design choices for cash assistance: how to structure payments, and how to choose recipients. Answer the questions below and the tool will suggest a practical option for both choices. The questions are based on a rapid review of high-quality evidence about what works well in different contexts. The tool is intended to provoke a structured discussion and support your judgment, not replace it; it is not a substitute for context analysis, safeguarding, or community engagement.

Rule of thumb

One payment works best when people need enough money at once to do something meaningful. Several payments work best when the main job is to cover regular costs over time. A mixed option works best when both are true.

Rule of thumb

A community-led model should clear a high bar. If local knowledge is strong and safeguards are strong, it can work well. If favouritism, tension, or safety risks are high, keep stronger control with the aid organisation. In many settings, a shared approach is the safest middle ground.


Regardless of the approach you choose, be transparent in communicating how decisions are made and what people can do if they have concerns or want to appeal.
